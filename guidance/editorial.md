## The displacement district
Stance: AGAINST treating Santa Fe's historic overlay as neighborhood preservation — it is a displacement district that stores wealth, not people.
Why: The city's own Continuity & Connection report (2021): district population fell 26% (1980–2018) while the city grew 69%; 30% of district housing vacant not year-round vs 14% citywide; legacy families displaced by part-time owners, vacation rentals, and galleries; median district income passed the rest of the city; eastside-area median home ~$820k vs ~$428k city (2020). Latino share fell sharply. Preservation without people is a museum.
Talking points: second/third/fourth homes; STR epicenter; schools losing families; housing ripples so city jobs don't pencil; "all the Hispanics are going to move and the only thing that will remain is the Spanish street names" (resident, city report).
Framing hook: preservation without people is a museum, not a city.
Use only when HDRB / historic districts / design review is on this week's record.

## Original sin: costume over commons
Stance: AGAINST HDRB as enforcement of a 1912 tourism costume, not stewardship of residents or the commons.
Why: Continuity traces the ordinance to City Different boosterism — stylistic harmony, tourism, property values — not retaining legacy residents. Residents told the city the process "values aesthetics over people." We extend that to crumbling streets and sidewalks: curated "authentic decay" while bullnoses get votes.
Talking points: marketing ordinance; postcard vs sidewalk; heat/habitability vs stucco color; 1912 Santa Fe Style conformity; ordinance goal of "continued construction in historical styles."
Framing hook: style police for the postcard; neglect for the people on the sidewalk.
Use only when HDRB / historic districts is on this week's record.

## The ripple loop
Stance: AGAINST choking infill in the most walkable, best-served core — it exports growth, cost, and climate harm citywide.
Why: Blocking homes downtown pushes development south into car-dependent sprawl; housing costs ripple to every employer including the city; worse hiring → worse services → worse streets → more flight to the fringe → more lane-miles to maintain. Continuity admits preservation "played a role" in gentrification and recommends affordable housing inside districts — tacit admission the balance failed.
Talking points: anti-urbanism in the urban core; bullnose vote downtown = pothole and pickup truck south; city can't staff departments when housing binds before salary.
Framing hook: a bullnose vote downtown is a pothole and a pickup truck south.
Use only when HDRB / housing supply / land-use is on this week's record.

## Disneyland preservation (not real historic preservation)
Stance: AGAINST Santa Fe's themed-zoning model — Solvang/City Different logic, not preservation as most cities define it.
Why: Real preservation protects authentic fabric (National Register–style age, integrity, significance). Santa Fe prioritizes stylistic harmony and revivalism; requires new buildings in "historical styles"; regulates fake vigas, stucco-over-plywood, simulated divided light, bullnose radius on ~6,500 structures (12% of city land). Take real buildings, stucco over them, call them "contributing," mandate costume on new work — gentrification loop, not conserved communities. Few U.S. districts force subjective style details on every new home; Santa Fe does.
Talking points: Solvang not Charleston; revivalism not integrity; "contributing" ≠ nationally historic; performance of heritage vs fabric; raises build/maintenance cost → displacement.
Framing hook: it's not preservation — it's a theme park overlay on the most walkable part of the city.
Use only when HDRB / design review / Santa Fe Style is on this week's record.

# Our positions

## Historic Districts / design review (HDRB and kin — case week)

Why: These boards judge whether something looks right, not whether it serves the city. They would bless harm if it wore the right style and kill something good (housing, needed public works, ADUs, casitas) if it didn't.
Talking points: stake vs style reply; beauty-contest quotes; process-for-process; bullnose/window-reveal theater; 515 Peralta whiplash; Don Gaspar casitas vs state office; same veto theater that blocks homes.
Framing hook: style police, not stewards of outcomes.
Use only when HDRB is on this week's record.

## Affordable Housing Trust Fund as currently structured
Stance: AGAINST continued AHTF operation without structural reform that accumulates real capital for construction.
Why: The fund operated as a reimbursement pass-through until FY25 — no corpus ever accumulated. FY24 balance went negative (–$97,250.71); a $3.07M BAR was needed to backfill the gap. Finance Director Oster confirmed this on record Dec 10, 2025.
Talking points: flagship housing fund with no money in it; empty corpus; negative balance; on-record admission; reform means capital that actually buys homes.
Framing hook: the city's flagship housing fund has no money in it, and its own Finance Director admitted why.
Use only when this week's meetings actually cover AHTF / these facts.

## FY26 AHTF award round's "1,670 units" claim
Stance: AGAINST accepting the headline number at face value.
Why: Only 8 of ~1,670 units are cleanly bought by AHTF dollars under a ≥10%-of-capital-stack attribution rule; the rest ride a LIHTC deal that didn't need the money and a $1.5M subsidy for a 60%-market-rate subdivision.
Talking points: demand honest attribution; expose paper units; city credit for housing it didn't fund is fraud-adjacent progress theater.
Framing hook: the city is taking credit for housing it didn't fund.
Use only when this week's meetings actually cover unit-count / award claims.

## Fee-in-lieu pricing (Bill 2026-11)
Stance: AGAINST the fee-in-lieu system as currently designed — NOT because the fee is too low, but because it funds a broken pipeline that has failed to produce housing supply. ALSO AGAINST treating higher fees alone, payment-deferral schemes, or NIMBY process as the fix. The gospel is units built.
Why: The city already raised the buyout high enough to effectively force on-site affordable construction; housing starts collapsed and rents rose — a policy failure most officials and residents don't acknowledge. SFHP delivered 4.1% affordable units against a 15–20% mandate — a compliance failure, not evidence the fee was too low. Pre-2016 (zero rental construction 2005–2016) shows choking supply kills homes. Fee-in-lieu dollars flow into AHTF, which has largely subsidized rentals not construction (see demand-side subsidy spending). OAH policies often make building harder and more expensive (see Office of Affordable Housing). Neither "raise the fee and call it done" nor "kill the fee for process theater" is the answer: build.
When this topic is on the agenda, also apply in our take: Affordable Housing Trust Fund, Demand-side subsidy spending, Office of Affordable Housing.
Talking points: punitive fees already killed supply; trust fund pays rent not homes; OAH chokes building; supply is the goal; do not both-sides NIMBYs who want less housing; city's own data prove supply failure; most residents don't know this history played out here.
Framing hook: the city is rearranging fee schedules on a pipeline that already choked supply and mostly pays rent — not building homes.
Forbidden framings: NEVER welcome, overdue, step in the right direction, previous fee was a joke, too low, cheap buyout, critical move to make developers contribute, laughably low fee.
How to use transcript quotes: Officials/staff (including Chavez) arguing the fee is too low or pushing higher fees — report what they said, but our take REBUTS; they are part of the broken system's own narrative. Payment-deferral "loan program" quotes are useful to expose dysfunction, NOT to endorse the hike.
Use only when fee-in-lieu / SFHP is actually on this week's record.

## Office of Affordable Housing
Stance: AGAINST current OAH policy direction without structural reform toward supply-side outcomes.
Why: OAH policies often make housing harder and more expensive to build, not easier. The office's own reporting has shown it has largely been ineffective if not outright damaging to affordability — sad irony for an "affordable housing" office.
Talking points: OAH blocks supply; own reporting shows failure; policies raise build cost; fee-in-lieu and AHTF sit under this broken apparatus.
Framing hook: the office named for affordable housing makes building harder.
When fee-in-lieu / SFHP / AHTF is on the agenda, weave OAH into the our-take even if OAH is not named in this week's transcript.
Use only when affordable housing policy, fee-in-lieu, SFHP, or AHTF is on this week's record.

## Displacement counted as "progress"
Stance: AGAINST the city's use of a shrinking rental gap as a success metric when the gap shrinks via displacement.
Why: Consolidated Plan data shows the gap shrank via displacement of low-income renters, not unit delivery, while renter cost burden rose over the same period.
Talking points: displacement is not a win; demand actual unit delivery; cost burden up = failure.
Framing hook: the city's own plan is quietly counting displaced renters as a win.
Use only when Consolidated Plan / rental-gap metrics are on this week's record.

## Demand-side subsidy spending (rental assistance vs construction)
Stance: AGAINST current AHTF spending mix that pays rent instead of building in a supply-constrained market.
Why: Expenditure structure has no capital/construction line items — spending skews to demand-side subsidy, which tends to push rents up rather than down when supply is tight. Fee-in-lieu dollars fund this spending mix.
Cross-link: when fee-in-lieu is on the agenda, cite this in our take.
Talking points: trust fund meant to build mostly pays rent; add construction capital lines; demand-side cash ≠ housing supply.
Framing hook: the trust fund meant to build housing mostly pays rent instead.
Use only when AHTF spending mix is on this week's record.
