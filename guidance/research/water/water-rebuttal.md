# Water vs. housing permits (city research)

Use in **our take** only when this week's official storylines cover water, permits, LDC, general plan, or moratorium language from officials — never because of open-floor public comment.

## Stance

Moratorium demands to "wait for a water study" ignore work the city has already done or is doing through Santa Fe Forward and the LDC update. New housing built to current code is the most water-efficient residential development; legacy stock and outdoor irrigation dominate demand. If water were the real constraint, the lever is outdoor watering restrictions — not freezing residential permits.

## Talking points (add study names, dates, and numbers from your PDFs)

- City has conducted water supply and demand analyses as part of planning updates — cite specific study titles when added here.
- New development meets current plumbing and efficiency standards; per-unit demand is lower than legacy housing stock.
- Existing residential water use is dominated by outdoor irrigation on older properties, not by new permit approvals.
- A permit freeze does not reduce irrigation on existing homes; tighter outdoor watering rules would.
- General plan and LDC processes are the official track — process-as-moratorium is obstruction, not stewardship.
